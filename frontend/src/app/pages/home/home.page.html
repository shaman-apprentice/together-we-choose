Why your choice matters

Traditionally, the wife often adopted the husband's family name. But in our modern, emancipated society we can do better than making a choice only based on tradition.

Here are a few reasons, why your choice matters:

Names can carry identity, history, emotional and more - for you, your siblings, parents and grandparents. Your feelings, your partner's feelings and your family's feelings deserve respect. Your options will vary by country and region. Can you combine names or create a new one? What about legal implications for future children's surnames? Check local laws or ask your registrar. Although it is tradition for the wife to adopt the husband's family name, nobody should take that for granted. Respect each other's feelings and find compromises. Your reasons are individual and personal. There's no single right answer. So, start the conversation and discuss what matters to you.

Questions to discuss together

Here are some questions to consider and give you a good start:

How attached are you to your family name? Is it your nickname? Are there stories or traditions tied to it? Do you connect values from your parents with it, you're proud of? Does it feel connected to 'home'?” Has one family name a special nice sound to it? Which name is easier to spell? Are your names part of your professional identity or business? Do you publish, perform, or have recognition under your current name? If you plan to have children, learn how local rules affect their surnames. If you both keep your names, your children may be allowed only one. In everyday situations (doctor’s office, school), different surnames can sometimes be awkward. Is family heritage important to you? Is your family name meaningful to your parents or grandparents? Reflect on what matters to you. Be honest with each other. Embrace your uniqueness. Take your time.
